Why I build this way

Local-first isn’t a setting. It’s the whole idea.

I don’t build software that phones home, because I don’t think it should have to. If a tool can do its job entirely on your device, sending your data anywhere else isn’t a feature — it’s just a risk I’m asking you to carry for my convenience.

No accounts, for the same reason. An account is a password to lose, a database to breach, a login screen between you and the thing you already paid for. If the software doesn’t need to know who you are, it shouldn’t ask.

And no subscription for something you’ve already paid for once. What I build for you should still work in five years, without a monthly charge quietly keeping it alive.

Software should belong to the person using it — not to a server, not to an account, not to me.

Questions

Before you send a brief.

Why don't you take calls?

A call is easy to forget and hard to hold anyone to. A written brief and a recorded walkthrough cover the same ground, and both sides can always point back to exactly what was said.

Can you sign an NDA?

Yes — send it with your brief and I'll review and sign before any details change hands.

Why are prices fixed instead of quoted?

A quote invites negotiation and scope creep. A published price means you know the number before you write to me, and I've already thought through what it actually takes to deliver it.

Can you maintain my software after it ships?

That's what the Keeper Plan is for — capped, pausable, ongoing care for anything I built. It's optional, and it's the one exception to buy-once pricing.

What happens if Apple rejects the app?

I handle resubmission as part of the build — that's the review clock belonging to Apple, not an extra charge to you.

Can I own the source code?

Yes. Once a Quiet Build ships, the code is yours outright — no ongoing license, no dependency on me to keep using it.

Do you use AI to build?

Yes, as a technical collaborator — the same way I build the Eden Apps lineup. Every decision, every line that ships, and everything it's responsible for is still mine.

Why local software instead of a SaaS subscription?

Because most of what gets subscribed to didn't need to be. A tool you use once a week shouldn't cost you monthly forever — buy it once, own it, and it's still yours in five years.
